**Conventions.** Underlined symbols denote RMS fundamental phasors; a few early equations, such as (8.1) and (8.4), use plain symbols where the phasor meaning is clear from the context. Physical circuit equations are per phase, so $\underline{V}\,\underline{I}^{*}$ is multiplied by 3 for balanced three-phase power, while per-unit equations already use a common three-phase base. With balanced line-to-line RMS voltages, $V_{LL,1}V_{LL,2}/X$ gives three-phase power directly, but a current still needs the factor $\sqrt{3}$. Series injection is written as a voltage rise, $\underline{V}_{s}' = \underline{V}_{s} + \underline{V}_{pq}$, the opposite of a series-drop convention. Converter powers $P_{se}$ and $P_{sh}$ are positive from converter to AC network; the IPC powers $Q_{S}$ and $Q_{R}$ are positive into the controller at their own terminals. Angles inside derivatives and integrals are in radians. Sections 8.1 to 8.7 form the core of the chapter. Section 8.8 is intended for advanced and research study and assumes familiarity with small-signal control, impedance methods and protection.
